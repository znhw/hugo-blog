During my early years of building websites, [Jekyll](https://jekyllrb.com/) was the first static site generator that I got to know and I believe it’s the most popular one among developers at the time, partly because it’s also the default ‘engine’ for GitHub Pages. 

Even though I did not use Jekyll to create websites for clients, I’d still spend time watching YouTube tutorials to build my own personal sites which never got published. And the reason is that Jekyll, just like Hugo, is designed specifically for content-focused or blogging sites. 

My younger self just did not have the brain cells to spit out content.

Now that I’m more mature personally and professionally, I decided to once again revive the ambition of building my own personal site. One of my goals for 2026 is to write at least 5 articles (ideally technical content), I could’ve just use substack and medium but that wouldn’t align much with my “developer” identity. 

[Nextjs](https://nextjs.org/) is the default stack for me when developing websites, and I intentionally choose **NOT** to use it, so that I make myself learn a new framework or coding language. Me being an ambitious developer, I ended up choosing [Hugo](https://gohugo.io/), because it’s built using [Golang](https://go.dev/), and hopefully gets me a step closer to working in Google. 🤞

Unlike Nextjs, which has Server-side Rendering capabilities and more advanced framework to develop complex full-stack applications, Hugo purely a static-site generator. 

As mentioned above that Hugo is meant for content-heavy / blogging sites, because it comes pre-built with features for managing content and the site’s template, almost like plug-and-play. Whereas, in Nextjs, the folder structures and naming can be flexible, the developer has to figure it out how to connect everything. Besides, in Hugo you must install a theme, and change the files’ code for your own design. 

Some people say Hugo’s learning curve is steeper, I’d challenge that notion, it only took me a couple of days to get a hang of it. Even though, the dev community is way smaller than JS based frameworks, the [official docs](https://gohugo.io/documentation/) are written pretty well. 

And some Youtube tutorials that helped speed up my learning: 
[Hugo Intro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZFL09qhKi5I) <br> and
[Hugo Content Organization](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0GZxidrlaRM)


While I appreciate the power of AI, I did not use AI at all for learning Hugo and building this site, I took the "old-school" approach by using stackoverflow, tutorials, and docs to figure this out. If I had used AI then I would not understand the technology, and also because I believe the ***process of building something is as important as the end result***.
